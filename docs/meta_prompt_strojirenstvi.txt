Use the uploaded hand-drawn mechanical sketch as the authoritative source and convert it into a professional ISO-style mechanical engineering drawing.

Primary objective:
Create a clean, formal, standardized 2D mechanical drawing sheet that looks like it was prepared by an experienced CAD drafter from an imperfect hand sketch.

Source interpretation rules:
- Read the sketch and all handwritten notes, labels, arrows, dimensions, symbols, and descriptions around it as engineering evidence.
- Preserve all geometry, proportions, feature positions, relationships between views, and design intent that are clearly present.
- If parts of the sketch are incomplete, unclear, inconsistent, or missing, infer the missing information conservatively from:
  1. visible geometry,
  2. handwritten notes,
  3. symmetry,
  4. repeated features or patterns,
  5. standard machinable forms,
  6. normal mechanical drafting logic,
  7. standard ISO drawing conventions.
- Complete only what is strongly implied.
- If exact values are not explicitly supported, do not invent precise dimensions, tolerances, threads, fits, or manufacturing notes.
- When uncertainty remains, choose the simplest mechanically reasonable interpretation and keep the result professional and manufacturable.

Drawing style and output:
- Produce a precise orthographic mechanical drawing on a clean white sheet.
- Use crisp black CAD-like linework only.
- No sketch texture, paper texture, shadows, photorealism, painterly effects, decorative graphics, branding, watermark, or artistic stylization.
- Normalize rough hand-drawn geometry into precise mechanical geometry without changing the part design.

Apply ISO-style drafting conventions:
- visible edges: continuous thick lines
- hidden edges: thin dashed lines
- centerlines and axes: thin chain lines
- dimension lines, extension lines, leaders, and hatching: thin lines
- section hatching only where logically necessary
- clear line-weight hierarchy
- clean spacing
- accurate view alignment
- balanced sheet composition

Mechanical drawing requirements:
- Establish the main/front view from the sketch’s primary view.
- Add aligned top, side, section, or detail views only when clearly supported by the sketch or needed to express the part correctly.
- Add center marks for holes, bores, shafts, circular features, and symmetry where appropriate.
- Standardize circles, arcs, fillets, chamfers, slots, steps, and hole patterns into precise drafting geometry.
- Preserve all clearly indicated holes, cutouts, radii, chamfers, steps, axes, and repeated features.
- Use millimeters unless another unit is explicitly indicated.
- Include dimensions only when they are explicitly present or can be reliably derived from the handwritten notes or obvious geometric intent.
- Do not fabricate unsupported numeric dimensions.

Text handling:
- Convert handwritten notes into clean, legible technical annotations.
- Preserve important engineering notes, but standardize wording into concise formal drafting language.
- Keep text minimal, precise, and readable.
- Do not add random labels, fake standards references, fake part numbers, or extra notes.

Sheet formatting:
- Include a clean drawing border.
- Include a professional title block in the bottom-right corner.
- Title block fields:
  DRAWING TITLE
  PART / ITEM
  SCALE
  DATE
  REV
  UNIT: mm
- Include a projection symbol if appropriate.
- Include a scale note if appropriate.

Critical constraints:
- preserve the original mechanical design intent
- preserve the relative location and size relationships of all major features
- preserve clearly shown geometry exactly
- infer only what is strongly implied
- do not creatively redesign the part
- do not add unsupported components or features
- do not output a concept sketch or presentation board
- do not use perspective unless absolutely necessary to normalize the sketch into orthographic form

Final quality target:
The final image must look like a professional, standards-aware, workshop-ready mechanical technical drawing created from a rough hand sketch and handwritten shop notes, with conservative engineering completion and no speculative invention.
